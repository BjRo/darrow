# GPT-5.6 routing and intent-router calibration — 2026-08-08

Status: **exploratory, N=1, not decision-bearing**

This calibration tests the GPT-5.6 routing chart as a hypothesis after making
the parent `pursue-goal` skill the canonical source of workflow, risk, and
reasoning-demand selection. It compares raw native goal mode, Darrow's active
`fast`/`standard`/`deep` policy, and an isolated candidate policy. The active
`routes.tsv` remains unchanged. The machine-readable companion is
[`2026-08-08-gpt-5.6-routing-router-n1.json`](2026-08-08-gpt-5.6-routing-router-n1.json).

## Corpus and routes

All tasks use fixed, human-authored OSS snapshots documented in the corpus
manifest. Each implementation passed its hidden behavior contract and focused
upstream checks.

| Case            | Shape                        | Raw native control | Active selection                                    | Candidate selection                                 |
| --------------- | ---------------------------- | ------------------ | --------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Cobra lifecycle | behavior-preserving refactor | Sol/medium         | `standard`, refactor, routine, Sol/medium           | `scaled`, refactor, routine, Terra/medium           |
| Commander env   | multi-file new feature       | Sol/medium         | `standard`, implement-feature, elevated, Sol/medium | `scaled`, implement-feature, elevated, Terra/medium |
| Express links   | localized security bug       | Sol/medium         | `standard`, fix-bug, elevated, Sol/medium           | `routine`, fix-bug, high, Luna/high                 |

The Express active-policy selection is a real classifier miss: header-injection
hardening crosses a security boundary and therefore requires `high` risk even
though the coding route can remain routine. The candidate selected the intended
independent combination: Luna/high execution with high verification.

## Oracle corrections and exclusions

The suite was authored while the intent policy was still changing. Its result
files preserve the original hidden expectations rather than rewriting history.
The reviewed interpretation corrects three expectations from the canonical
policy, without using task outcomes:

- Cobra is `routine` risk because the scoped refactor is reversible and has
  limited blast radius; its multi-file implementation is `scaled`, not routine.
- Commander under the active policy is clear bounded work and therefore
  `standard`/Sol-medium, not `deep`/Sol-high.
- The ambiguous migration fixture is `elevated` because it changes persisted
  formats and multiple consumers, but its clear localized coding route is
  `routine`/Luna-high; migration and elevated risk do not imply scaled routing.

The main suite's Commander native cell used the earlier Sol/high control. It is
excluded and replaced by the dedicated Sol/medium rerun from
`2026-08-08T17-42-05-230Z`. A full suite at
`2026-08-08T15-54-49-066Z` predates the strengthened canonical router and is
excluded entirely.

## Bounded conclusion

The canonical parent router and config-driven route application now demonstrate
the intended separation: workflow controls sequence, risk controls verification,
and routing controls reasoning kind and scale. The focused evals also cover bug
versus intentional change, refactor versus mechanical, change-feature versus
migration, localized high-risk coding, and difficult routine-risk diagnosis.

Do not promote the candidate mappings from N=1. The candidate did not improve
aggregate latency or token volume, but it is estimated to reduce list-price cost
by about 72% while all three task contracts still passed. Terra/medium on larger
straightforward work is the strongest combined quality, latency, token, and cost
signal and merits N>=3 replication against active and raw-native controls.
Luna/high's Express run was much slower and more token-heavy but substantially
cheaper; additional localized and quality-sensitive cases are needed before it
can replace the active standard route.
